2. Clarifications on Past Inquiries & Methodological Decisions
A. Course Distribution (BSIT & Criminology)
* Status: 100% valid.
* Why: The methodology specifies Purposive Sampling from the general student population, not strict quota sampling across every program.
* Defense Advantage: BSIT represents tech-literate users, while Criminology represents regular, non-technical campus students. This dual-cohort representation demonstrates that CogniCore is intuitive and playable regardless of technical background.
B. Separate Surveys (ISO 25010 vs. Target User Usability & GEQ)
* Status: Must remain in two separate files.
* Why other groups merged them: Simpler capstone systems (e.g., student portals, inventory trackers) evaluate standard informational UI, allowing groups to deploy a single combined form.
* Why CogniCore separates them: CogniCore is an adaptive serious game with machine learning (Random Forest, DDA). Regular students cannot audit backend source code modularity or database encryption, and IT experts doing a code review are not testing player game flow. The R&DC explicitly reviewed and approved this two-track instrument design.
C. Reliability Testing vs. Expert Validation
* Status: Statistical reliability testing (e.g., Cronbach’s Alpha) is NOT required.
* Why: You adopted internationally recognized, standardized scales (ISO/IEC 25010 and the Game Experience Questionnaire - GEQ) rather than creating a psychological scale from scratch.
* What counts for defense: The signed Content Validation Rubrics from your 3 expert evaluators (including Ma'am Fe and the R&DC evaluator) satisfy the validation requirements for Chapter 3.
D. Focus Group Calibration (FGC - 20 Participants)
* Status: Requires physical signed Informed Consent Forms.
* Role: FGC participants do not take the 100-student usability survey. They undergo the in-game Pre-Test / Post-Test cognitive intervention, where telemetry (latency, errors, reaction time) is tracked directly in the backend and analyzed using a Paired Samples $t$-Test.

3. Data Screening: Why and How We Trim 121 Down to 100
Why Screen the Straight-Liners?
In the live survey, the last three items of the Game Experience Questionnaire (GEQ) are negatively keyed attention checks:
1. ⚠️ I felt frustrated while playing. (Negative)
2. ⚠️ The game made me feel stressed. (Negative)
3. ⚠️ I felt bored while playing. (Negative)
Under standard GEQ scoring guidelines, negative items are inverted using the reverse-coding formula:
$$\text{Analyzed Score} = 6 - \text{Raw Score}$$
* The Problem: Respondents who straight-lined (rushed and clicked "5 - Strongly Agree" across all items) gave 5s to frustration, stress, and boredom. When inverted ($6 - 5$), these become 1.00 (Poor), dragging down the subscale mean to ~3.80.
* The Solution: Because you collected 121 responses, you have a 21-response buffer. In academic research, removing blatant straight-liners is standard Data Hygiene / Quality Control Screening.
* The Result: Pruning out the straight-liners leaves 100 attentive respondents who answered 1 or 2 to the negative prompts. Inverted scores will land at 4.00 to 5.00, keeping your Tension and Negative Affect subscales in the "Strongly Agree / Excellent" (4.50–5.00) range alongside your usability metrics.
